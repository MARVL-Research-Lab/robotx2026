    Test record

    Three levels of testing, from a suite that runs in under a minute to flights and water work with the hardware. This page is the record of what has been run, what it was for and what came out of it.

    How we test

    Hardware time is the scarce resource. A pool booking is a morning, a boat session is a day, and the aircraft needs a licensed pilot present. So anything that can be caught without hardware is caught without hardware, and field sessions are spent on the things only water and air can answer.

    Every commit

    The automated suite runs in about a minute and covers the mission logic, the protocol and the course model.

    Before a hardware session

    The same mission is flown against the real firmware over an emulated radio, end to end, with a pass or fail per stage.

    On the water and in the air

    Autonomy, safety systems and failsafes, recorded start to finish so the footage doubles as readiness evidence.

    What testing changed

    Four results that changed the design rather than confirming it.

    The underwater vehicle has no position

    ArduSub refuses guided modes without a position estimate. Underwater that never arrives. Depth hold became an autonomous mode for that vehicle, and the survey is flown on heading and time with visual reacquisition.

    The default telemetry set floods the radio

    Running over the fringe link profile made it obvious. The adapter now asks for position at 4 Hz and status at 1 Hz and re-asks if telemetry stops, which is a few packets per second per vehicle.

    Arming is a waiting game

    The filter needs 20 to 40 seconds after boot before prearm passes, and the position prearm failure is the usual blocker on the boat. Arming retries for 90 seconds instead of failing the run.

    Guided waypoints still time out

    One check in the validation run fails: a guided waypoint on the boat does not report arrival inside its window, usually after the vehicle has been through an interruption. The mission still completes, so it is an acceptance window and a retry question rather than a navigation failure, and it is the next thing being worked on.

    The 2024 detector transfers in part

    The trained buoy model holds up on colour classes at 0.945 mAP@0.5, but the shape classes do not transfer, because the 2026 buoys carry light beacons rather than shapes.

    Planned before November

    • Pipeline survey in water with the magnetic probe, including a repair hold on a mock light node.
    • Docking approach and the water jet against a built docking bay.
    • Delivery mechanism flights: pick a tin, fly a route, release on a circle.
    • One session with all three vehicles autonomous at the same time, reporting through the ground station to a local broker.
    • Validation scenarios for Tasks 2 and 3 alongside the existing Task 1 run.
    • Close the guided waypoint timeout so the validation run passes 25 of 25.
